Executive Dashboard Summary All Phases
Overview: 101,766 hospital encounters analyzed. 11.16% were readmitted within 30 days. High-utilization patients (6+ prior visits) reach 25.51% readmission rate — 3× higher than patients with no prior visits.
Patient & Clinical Overview Phase 3
Three-panel view: Target distribution (53.9% not readmitted), readmission by race (small differences, interpret cautiously), and diagnosis burden showing both LOS and readmission rate increase significantly with more diagnoses.

🔍 Phase 3 – Exploratory Data Analysis (10 Basic Questions)
Q1: Readmission by Age Group Q1
Key finding: [20-30) age group has 14.24% readmission rate — the highest. Older patients (80-90) show 12.08%. The pattern is non-linear, likely reflecting disease management differences across life stages.
Q2: LOS by Medical Specialty Q2
Key finding: Nephrology (5.0d), Pulmonology (5.2d), and Surgery-Cardiovascular (5.8d) have the longest average stays. ObGyn (3.1d) and Urology (3.4d) are shortest. Note: 49% of specialty data is missing.
Q3: Emergency Visits vs Readmission ⭐ Top Signal
Strongest predictor: Patients with 4+ prior emergency visits have 28.54% readmission rate vs 10.47% for patients with none — nearly 3× higher. This is the most actionable risk factor.
Q4: Diabetes Medication vs Readmission Q4
Finding: Patients on diabetes medication: 11.63% readmit rate vs 9.60% without. Difference is modest — diabetes medication alone is not a strong predictor. Co-morbidities matter more.
Q5: Medication Change vs Readmission Q5
Finding: Changed medication → 11.82% readmit rate. No change → 10.59%. Patients with changed medications may have clinical instability at discharge.
Q6: Lab Procedures vs Readmission Q6
Finding: Readmission rate is relatively flat across lab procedure counts (~11%). Slight uptick at 80+ lab tests may reflect high-complexity monitoring cases.
Q7: Readmission by Race & Gender Q7
⚠️ Interpret cautiously: Caucasian 11.29%, AfricanAmerican 11.22%, Hispanic 10.41%, Asian 10.14%. Differences are very small. These cannot be causally attributed to race/gender — social determinants of health (SDOH) are absent from this dataset.
Q8: Weight vs Readmission 96.87% Missing
⚠️ Data limitation: Only 3,197 patients (3.13%) have weight data. Results are not representative and cannot be generalized. No conclusions should be drawn from this chart.
Q9: Medications vs Length of Stay Q9
Strong correlation: Low meds (1-5): 2.28d → Moderate (6-15): 3.50d → High (16-25): 5.04d → Very High (26+): 7.31 days. Medication burden is a clear proxy for clinical complexity.
Q10: Outpatient Visits vs Readmission Q10
Finding: 0 visits → 10.67%, 1 visit → 13.92%, 2 visits → 13.75%. Patients with some prior outpatient history show modestly higher readmission, possibly reflecting ongoing disease management needs.

Model Comparison (AUC + Recall) Phase 5
Random Forest achieves best ROC-AUC (0.6692) and balanced performance. Recall of 0.58 means it correctly identifies ~58% of actual readmissions — valuable for clinical triage even at this moderate accuracy level.
ROC Curves – All 3 Models Phase 5
All three models outperform random (diagonal line, AUC=0.50). The moderate AUC of ~0.67 is expected given the absence of key clinical features (vital signs, lab values, nursing notes) that would significantly improve predictions.
Confusion Matrix – Random Forest Phase 5
The matrix shows the trade-off between true positives (correctly identified readmissions) and false negatives (missed readmissions). With balanced class weights, the model catches more actual readmissions at the cost of some false alarms.
Feature Importance – Random Forest ⭐ Phase 5
Top drivers: number_inpatient (28.9%), discharge_disposition_id (13.2%), num_lab_procedures (6.6%), num_medications (6.3%), number_emergency (5.7%). Prior hospitalization history dominates — confirming that past utilization predicts future utilization.
Feature Importance – Detailed (Top 15) Phase 5
Clinical implication: The fact that prior inpatient visits (28.9%) is the #1 feature confirms that repeat hospitalizers are at highest risk. Discharge disposition (#2) shows that where patients go after hospitalization strongly predicts if they'll return. Clinical teams should prioritize these two signals at discharge planning.

💡 Phase 10 – Top 10 Key Business Insights
⭐ Insight 1: Emergency Visit History is the #1 Readmission Predictor Critical
Data: 4+ emergency visits → 28.54% readmission rate vs 10.47% for zero visits.
Business impact: A small group (939 patients with 4+ emergency visits) has nearly 3× the average risk.
Action: Implement post-discharge care management calls within 48h for any patient with 2+ prior emergency visits.
⭐ Insight 2: High-Risk Segment Has 24.4% Readmission Rate Critical
Data: 6,705 patients (6.6% of total) with high emergency + inpatient history → 24.35% readmit rate.
Business impact: This segment drives disproportionate readmissions despite being a small subset.
Action: Create a dedicated care management program specifically for Segment 2 patients.
Insight 3: 49% Medical Specialty Data is Missing Data Quality
Data: 49,949 of 101,766 encounters have no specialty recorded.
Business impact: Specialty-level analytics and staffing decisions are based on incomplete data.
Action: Mandate specialty documentation at every admission. Fix EHR workflow to require this field.
Insight 4: 96.87% Weight Data Missing Data Quality
Data: 98,569 of 101,766 weight values missing (coded as "?" in raw data).
Business impact: BMI-based risk stratification is impossible. Weight is clinically critical for diabetes patients.
Action: Integrate weight capture into EHR admission workflow. Track completion rate as a quality metric.
Insight 5: Medication Burden Predicts Length of Stay Operational
Data: Low meds (1-5): 2.28d → Very High (26+): 7.31 days. 3× difference in LOS.
Business impact: High-medication patients consume ~3× the bed-days of low-medication patients.
Action: Pharmacy-led medication reconciliation at discharge for patients with 20+ medications.
Insight 6: High-Utilization Patients Need Priority Discharge Planning Clinical
Data: High utilization (6+ visits): 25.51% readmit vs 8.18% for no prior visits.
Business impact: This 4.3% of patients (4,425 people) accounts for disproportionate hospital costs and bed usage.
Action: Auto-flag any patient with 6+ combined prior visits for enhanced discharge planning.
Insight 7: Diabetes Medication Alone is a Weak Predictor Clinical
Data: Diabetes med Yes: 11.63% vs No: 9.60%. Difference is small (~2%).
Business impact: Simply being on diabetes medication does not substantially predict readmission.
Action: Focus on diabetes patients WITH high emergency visit history — the combination is the real risk signal.
Insight 8: The ML Model Catches ~58% of Future Readmissions ML
Data: Random Forest achieves ROC-AUC 0.6692, Recall 0.5795.
Business impact: Even at moderate accuracy, flagging 58% of high-risk patients enables proactive intervention.
Action: Pilot the model in a staging environment. Set probability threshold based on care team capacity.
Insight 9: 3-5 Day Stays are the Largest Group (40.9%) Operational
Data: 41,646 patients stay 3-5 days — the single largest group. Readmit rate: 11.37%.
Business impact: Optimizing care transitions for this group has the largest system-wide volume impact.
Action: Implement structured discharge planning protocol starting at Day 2 for all moderate-stay patients.
Insight 10: Race/Gender Differences Are Clinically Small Equity
Data: Readmit rates: Caucasian 11.29%, AfricanAmerican 11.22%, Hispanic 10.41%, Asian 10.14%.
⚠️ Important: These small differences should NOT be used to deprioritize any group's care.
Action: Supplement with SDOH (income, housing, food security) data for responsible equity analysis.
